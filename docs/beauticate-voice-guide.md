# Beauticate Writing Voice Guide

*A reference for anyone writing, editing or approving content for Beauticate.*

---

## The Short Version

Elevated but chatty. Intelligent but accessible. Personal but authoritative. Think Vogue meets Refinery29, with the warmth of a trusted friend leaning in rather than a salesperson pitching.

If you only remember one thing: the insight or ritual comes first, the product is the quiet helper, never the point.

---

## 1\. Language & Spelling

- Australian/British English throughout. Never American spelling.  
  - colour, organise, favour, personalise, recognise, centre  
- No em dashes. Ever. Use a single hyphen if needed, or a comma.  
- No Oxford commas.  
- Hyphens are fine for compound words and asides.

---

## 2\. Sentence Craft

- Short sentences. Favour full stops over trailing clauses or pauses built with dashes and semicolons.  
- Write like a human journalist, not an AI. Vary rhythm, but keep it clean.  
- No fluff, no waffle, no over-the-top language, unless the piece is a deep personal or emotional one. In those pieces, warmth and depth are welcome and encouraged.  
- Avoid the word "genuinely." Find another way in, or cut it.  
- Never use bullet points in articles or editorial copy. Write in prose. (Reference documents like this one are the exception.)

---

## 3\. Tone by Format

**Articles and blog posts** Chatty and informal but stylish and elevated. SEO-engineered with relevant keywords woven naturally, never stuffed. Feature the brands specified, and suggest Australian-available alternatives where relevant.

**Instagram captions** Longer and storytelling when the content earns it. Short and punchy when the moment is straightforward. Screen text and caption copy must say different things. Screen text should pull people into the caption, not repeat it.

**Interview and personal pieces** Preserve the subject's or contributor's own voice, even when the piece is significantly expanded or restructured. Where possible, restore their original words as attributed quotes rather than paraphrasing them into the house voice.

---

## 4\. Off-Brand Phrases and Framing

Avoid:

- "Get your skin strong and glowing first"  
- "Swears by"  
- Positioning any product as an "accessible" or cheaper alternative — "accessible" reads as cheap and jars against the luxury register  
- Instructional or salesy framing of any kind  
- Judgement of women at any life stage. The tone is non-judgemental, always.

---

## 5\. Commercial Integration ("The Beauticate Edit")

Shop and affiliate mentions live inside the editorial voice invisibly. They should never feel like an ad break.

**Placement**

- Editor's notes are placed throughout the piece, not consolidated at the end.  
- Guideline: one note per 600 to 800 words of editorial.  
- Personal interview pieces: one note maximum.  
- Survey or roundup pieces can carry a full "heroes" block of recommendations.  
- Single-product reviews or sponsored content: restraint, or nothing at all.

**Sponsored content** Shop notes must stay entirely clear of the sponsor's product category.

**Contributor-bylined pieces** Always a courtesy check with the contributor before shop edits go live under their name.

**Box format** Standing label: "The Beauticate Edit". Length: roughly 4 to 6 sentences, never longer.

---

## 6\. Structure & SEO

- Strong, practical subheadings.  
- Single-paragraph intros. Get to the point, then open it up.  
- Weave E-E-A-T signals in naturally (expertise, credentials, sourcing) rather than bolting them on.  
- Headlines should be practical and searchable, not just clever.

---

## 7\. Sign-Offs

- Close PR and beauty industry contacts: usually "Sig x" or "Sigourney x". Read the thread and match the existing tone before defaulting.  
- Team communication: warm and casual.  
- Formal brand outreach: "Sigourney", no kiss.  
- When in doubt, mirror whatever's already being used in the thread.

---

## 8\. Formatting for Delivery

- Default to Google Docs-compatible formats: .docx or clean copy-paste text. No Microsoft Office file quirks.  
- Documents should be editable and shareable by the team, not just readable by Sig.  
- Solutions and systems should be executable by the wider team, not dependent on one person holding it all in their head.

---

*This guide reflects Beauticate's evolution from a traditional editorial platform into a full editorial-commerce ecosystem: Goop meets Net-a-Porter, but more credible, more human and more accessible. Every piece of writing should serve that mission — trusted, expert-led content that helps people look and feel their best.*
