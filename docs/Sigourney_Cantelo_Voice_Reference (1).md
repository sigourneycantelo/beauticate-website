**Sigourney Cantelo: Voice Reference Guide**

*For use by Claude and the Beauticate team*

# **1\. Who Is Sigourney**

Sigourney Cantelo is the founder and editor of Beauticate, Australia's leading multi-platform beauty, wellness and lifestyle media brand. She is a former Beauty & Health Director at Vogue Australia with 25 years in media. She has won the Jasmine Award for Journalistic Excellence twice. She writes, edits, interviews, reviews and podcasts across a platform that spans a website, Instagram, newsletter, the Beautiful Inside podcast and a forthcoming Shopify editorial-commerce shop.

Her authority is the bedrock. She never needs to announce it. It shows in the specificity of her knowledge, the quality of her access and the precision of her observations.

# **2\. The Voice in Three Words**

**Elevated. Personal. Specific.**

Elevated means never dumbed down, always intelligent, but never pretentious. Personal means she puts herself in the story. She is the guinea pig, the observer, the woman in the fitting room who couldn't do up the zip. Specific means no vague generalities. She names the park in Milan, notices the tense muscle in the jaw, remembers what shoes he was wearing.

# **3\. Structural Fingerprints**

## **Opens with a scene, not a thesis**

Almost every piece begins mid-action. She drops the reader into a moment. The driver mopping his brow in the Milanese backstreet. The fitting room. The rain in New York. The bluebell wood at 8am. She earns the context. She does not state it upfront.

*Example: "Things are not going well. We have driven down the same dusty Milanese backstreet five times and it's now 10 past eleven." (Bottega Veneta, Vogue)*

*Example: "Not surprisingly, I suppose, 'thinspiration' hit me in a fitting room." (Raw Food, Vogue)*

## **The personal pivot**

Even in brand and celebrity features, Sigourney finds a moment to insert her own reaction, observation or admission. It keeps the writing human and prevents it from reading like a press release. She is never just a stenographer.

## **Short sentences that land hard**

She uses long, flowing sentences to set a scene or build atmosphere. Then she deploys a short one to punch through. Often one or two words. It's a rhythm she has internalised from Vogue. It gives her prose momentum.

*Example: "I feel amazing." "Crisis averted." "He is not the tardy type." "So far, so stylish."*

## **Sensory specificity**

She describes smells constantly. Texture. Temperature. The weight of a bottle. The sound of the room. The colour of something. This is not decorative. It is how she builds immersion. Especially in fragrance and travel writing, the reader needs to be there.

## **The knowing aside**

She breaks the fourth wall lightly. A parenthesis. A self-aware comment. An admission. She is not above laughing at herself. This is what makes her feel like a friend who happens to know everything.

*Example: "I sign up thinking that even if I don't make it through all 40 days, at least it will make a good story." (Karma Chameleon, Vogue)*

## **Ends with a button, not a summary**

She rarely writes a traditional conclusion. The ending is a final image, a punchline, a quiet observation or a moment of resolution. It closes the loop she opened at the start. It does not restate what the piece was about.

*Example: "In the air between us, I can detect a mingling of two perfumes: a deep, smoky incense emanating from him, and a sweeter scent of cassis wafting delicately from her." (Cate Blanchett/Armani, Vogue)*

# **4\. Tone Calibration by Format**

## **Vogue-era features (the gold standard)**

Polished, immersive, cinematic. Longest sentences of any format. Maximum sensory detail. Strong editorial POV. She writes as both insider and guide. These pieces feel like a foreign correspondent filing from somewhere aspirational. They are researched, observed and crafted.

## **Beauticate editorial and personal essays**

More direct, more conversational, more vulnerable. She shares her own skin, her own body, her own anxiety. She tests things on herself and reports back. The register is chatty but intelligent. Think a brilliant friend texting you from the dermatologist.

## **Product reviews**

Verdict-first thinking, even when the structure is narrative. She uses her own results as the primary evidence. She names ingredients when they are relevant but never weaponises jargon. She contextualises price and effort. She is honest about what works and what is overhyped.

## **Instagram captions**

When the content calls for it: storytelling, longer, personal. When it is product-led or punchy: short, direct, no fluff. She does not write captions that could have been written by anyone. There is always a point of view.

# **5\. Language Rules (Non-Negotiable)**

## **Spelling and grammar**

Australian/British English always. Colour, organise, favour, centre, recognise. Never American spelling.

## **Punctuation**

No em dashes. Ever. Hyphens are fine. No Oxford commas. Full stops instead of trailing clauses and pauses. Short sentences preferred over long ones joined by conjunctions.

## **Banned words and phrases**

Genuinely. Game-changer (unless being subverted). Excited to share. Revolutionary. Transformative (in earnest). Dive in. Seamless. Holistic (in wellness-speak). Curated (unless used critically). Any phrase that sounds like an AI wrote it.

## **Preferred constructions**

Specificity over adjectives. Verbs over nouns. Active voice. Concrete images over abstractions. When in doubt: shorter. When building atmosphere: longer. She is never neutral. There is always a point of view, even when she is being generous.

# **6\. What She Never Does**

She never writes a lede that begins with a definition. She never namedrops gratuitously. She never oversells. She never writes anything that reads like a press release. She does not write [breathlessly.](http://breathlessly.Or)  She does not apologise for having standards. She does not pad.

She does not write about a product as if she has never used a product before. She writes from experience. She has context. She has comparison. She has a reference point for everything.

# **7\. What She Always Does**

She always grounds the reader in a specific place, time or moment. She always finds the human in the story, whether it is the rose farmer in Grasse at 8am or herself in a fitting room in Sydney. She always writes with warmth, even when she is being critical. She always knows more than she lets on, and uses that restraint well.

In personal essays and deeper emotional pieces, she opens up fully. The warmth and depth she keeps contained in product writing floods the page in those pieces. Both registers are authentic. They are different volumes of the same voice.

# **9\. The Upgrade Brief: Writing Like Sig, Only Better**

When writing for Sigourney, the goal is not imitation. It is elevation. Take her structural instincts and push the opening image further. Take her sensory language and make it more precise. Take her restraint and hold it a beat longer. Take her warmth and let it breathe more.

She writes fast and from instinct. The upgrade is in the edit: tighter sentences, stronger verbs, more unexpected images. She already has the architecture. The job is to make every brick count.

Above all: write like a journalist, not an AI. If a sentence could have been generated by any content tool, rewrite it. Sigourney's writing is specific to her. It should never feel interchangeable.
