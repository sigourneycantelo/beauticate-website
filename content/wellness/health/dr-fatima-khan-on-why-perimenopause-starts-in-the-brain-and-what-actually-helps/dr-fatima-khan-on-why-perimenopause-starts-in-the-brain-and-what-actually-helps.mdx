Anxious. Foggy. Not sleeping. And your blood tests come back fine. If that's you, you're not imagining it, and Dr Fatima Khan says it's a story she hears from so many women in their early 40s, when nobody is thinking about menopause yet.

I know this one from the inside. I was told it was my mental health. Looking back, it was perimenopause. It's exactly why we make Beautiful Inside, to have the conversations that don't get enough airtime, and why I wanted Fatima on the show.

Fatima is [one of Australia's leading voices in women's hormone health](https://www.drfatimakhan.com/). She trained in psychiatry, obstetrics and gynaecology and general practice, and founded The Menopause Clinic in Melbourne. Her new book, [*Hormone Health Is Mental Health*](https://link.amazon/B0apXaURc), is changing how women and doctors think about perimenopause. You can follow her on [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/drfatimakhan_/).

<QuickAnswer question="Can perimenopause start in your brain before you get hot flushes?">
Dr Fatima Khan says yes. She explains that the first signs of perimenopause often show up in the brain in your early 40s, as anxiety, fog and poor sleep, well before the first hot flush. Because most women are waiting for hot flushes around 50, a whole group of women aged 40 to 47 gets missed.
</QuickAnswer>

## Why it starts in the brain

Most of us have been told to look for hot flushes. Fatima's point is that for many women the first signs are mental: the anxiety, the brain fog, the rage nobody warned you about. She explains how falling progesterone takes away the body's natural calm, which is why it can feel as though your brain is betraying you.

## Why it's so easy to misread

Because those symptoms look like other things, women are often told it's stress or that it's all in their head. Fatima talks through how perimenopause can look like other conditions, including ADHD, and why that's such a blind spot.

## Track it before you talk to your GP

Her practical advice is one I wish I'd had: note your symptoms for three months before you see your GP. Her book has scripts for exactly what to say. It's the difference between walking out with a pat on the head and walking out with a plan. She also reminds us that this is general information, so talk to your own doctor about what's right for you.

## The fundamentals, and protecting your sleep

Fatima keeps coming back to the foundations: nutrition, movement, sleep, stress and social connection. On sleep she's refreshingly simple. Phones go in another room after 9pm, and you do a brain dump before bed. And the bigger permission slip: slow down and stop performing.
