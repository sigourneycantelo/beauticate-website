## TBH, I Wasn't Sure I Would Actually Use It

I got aboard the sauna bandwagon knowing it would be good for me. I have arthritis in my back, neck and shoulders. After a long day at a desk, the stiffness that sets in is the kind that used to send me straight to the bath, because nothing else eased the pain.

However, I wondered whether I would be bothered with the whole sweaty sauna shebang when the said tiredness actually set in. I was worried that the novelty would wear off and it would become one of those beautiful, expensive things that does nothing but gather dust and quiet judgement.

Three months later, I can tell you: I use it constantly. More than I expected, at least a few times a week. More than I use almost any other wellness practice in my life right now. Because it works and it just feels sooo good.

## What It's Done for My Back

The science behind this is not woo. Infrared heat penetrates tissue more deeply than a hot bath, reaching the muscles and joints rather than just warming the surface. What it's doing, at a cellular level, is activating heat shock proteins, the body's own repair crew. Heat shock protein 70 (HSP70) surges during a sauna session and is thought to suppress inflammatory signalling pathways and help resolve the chronic inflammation that drives conditions like arthritis. Some clinical studies have reported that infrared therapy can lower inflammatory markers including IL-6 and TNF-alpha, and ease pain scores in people with chronic back pain compared with control groups.

For me, the proof is practical. I get in, I do some gentle standing forward folds and seated yoga stretches in the warmth (the heat loosens everything so quickly, in a way that stretching cold never does) and I get out feeling like a different person. The muscle in my upper back that spends all day locked around my shoulder blade unclenches. The base of my spine, which had started to feel fused by five o'clock, moves again.

I used to say the bath was the thing I couldn't live without. The sauna is better.

## It's Also Doing Something for My Skin

I didn't buy the sauna for beauty reasons, but I started noticing a few things that I can now categorically say were not from skincare.

And the research is encouraging: infrared heat is thought to drive blood to the surface, delivering oxygen and nutrients directly to skin cells. Fibroblasts, the cells responsible for collagen and elastin production, are believed to respond to that increased circulation. The heat shock proteins activated during a session may also help protect skin cells from oxidative stress, one of the primary drivers of premature ageing. And the deep sweating that happens in a far infrared sauna clears pores of sebum and accumulated pollutants in a way that topical skincare can't match.

My skin looks clearer. It has that post-exercise glow, except I didn't have to exercise. Research suggests cortisol, the stress hormone that accelerates ageing, triggers breakouts and disrupts the skin barrier, drops during a sauna session.

![Sigourney Cantelo flushed and glowing after a session in her Nook Elite infrared sauna](/content/wellness/biohacking/nook-elite-infrared-sauna-review/post-sauna-glow.jpg)

Research also suggests improvements in sleep persist for one to two nights after each session, and we all know that sleep is when the skin's repair work actually happens.

Collagen loss accelerates in women over forty. Anything that may stimulate collagen synthesis (not promise it, stimulate it) is worth knowing about.
