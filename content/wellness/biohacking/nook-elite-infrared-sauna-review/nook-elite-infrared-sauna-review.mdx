## TBH, I Wasn't Sure I Would Actually Use It

I got aboard the sauna bandwagon because so many people I know swear by theirs. After a long day at a desk, what I want is somewhere warm and quiet to switch off, which used to mean the bath.

However, I wondered whether I would be bothered with the whole sweaty sauna shebang when the said tiredness actually set in. I was worried that the novelty would wear off and it would become one of those beautiful, expensive things that does nothing but gather dust and quiet judgement.

Three months later, I can tell you: I use it constantly. More than I expected, several times a week. More than I use almost any other wellness practice in my life right now. Because it just feels sooo good.

![Sigourney Cantelo smiling in the open doorway of her Nook Elite infrared sauna, holding the door handle](/content/wellness/biohacking/nook-elite-infrared-sauna-review/nook-elite-door-open-smile.jpg)

## How I Use It

I get in, I do some gentle standing forward folds and seated yoga stretches in the warmth (stretching in there feels lovely in a way that stretching in a cool room never does) and I get out feeling relaxed and like a different person.

I used to say the bath was the thing I couldn't live without. The sauna is better.

## A Ritual That Actually Sticks

I go in at the end of the day most often, but when I'm organised and I exercise I use it in the mornings, and then jump in the pool. It's cold enough (for me at least!) to do the job of a cold plunge. The contrast is extraordinary. Your body comes alive in a way that no amount of coffee can replicate.

What I didn't anticipate was how the Nook would become a kind of sanctuary. The NookTone Bluetooth audio system means I can connect my phone without touching it once I'm inside. I listen to Enya and Deep Forest and other fun nineties chill-out.

My ayurvedic doctor at Escape Haven Retreat in Bali told me to listen to Gayatri Mantra to balance my chakras. I like the [Deva Premal version](https://open.spotify.com/track/1kYeYUOjvEX9Vg3Tb8VIHR?si=9ee093a2565947cb). Sometimes I sit in complete silence. And yes, okay, sometimes I doom scroll or draft social posts. The point is: it's forced downtime. Even fifteen minutes at the end of a brutal day where I sit in there and breathe: it counts. Coco, my puppy, tries to join me every single time. I have to usher her out, because, no animals or children, but I respect her ambition.
