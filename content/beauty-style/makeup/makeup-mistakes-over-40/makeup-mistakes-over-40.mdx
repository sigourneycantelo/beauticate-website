## Your Skin Is the Star

If there's one thing I want someone to notice when they look at you, it's your skin…not your foundation.

Healthy-looking skin has never gone out of fashion.

In fact, I don't think a completely flat, matte complexion has ever been the most flattering finish, particularly once we reach our forties. As we age, our skin naturally loses moisture, elasticity and luminosity. Taking every bit of shine away doesn't make skin look polished, it often makes it look tired.

I explain it to my clients using a simple analogy.

Imagine two beautiful pieces of timber furniture.

One has just been polished. Light bounces off it beautifully. It looks rich, fresh and cared for.

The other has a layer of dust sitting over the top. The furniture underneath is exactly the same, but because nothing is reflecting the light, it appears dull, flat and lifeless.

Our skin behaves in a similar way.

Hydrated skin reflects light. It looks healthy, bouncy and vibrant.

<PullQuote>Hydrated skin reflects light. It looks healthy, bouncy and vibrant.</PullQuote>

Skin that's dehydrated, overloaded with powder or covered in layers of matte product loses that natural luminosity, making the complexion appear grey, flat and older than it really is.

That's why I believe skincare isn't separate from makeup.

It is makeup.

## Skin Prep Is the New Foundation

Women often ask me what foundation I recommend, but more often than not, my answer starts with skincare.

Because if your skin is dehydrated, rough or covered in a layer of dead skin cells, even the most beautiful foundation won't perform the way it's meant to.

One of the most overlooked steps is [regular exfoliation](/beauty-style/skin-care/dr-dennis-gross-on-why-you-have-to-exfoliate-and-how-to-do-it-properly/).

If dead skin cells remain sitting on the surface, your expensive serums and moisturisers are largely nourishing skin that's ready to shed anyway. Gentle exfoliation allows fresh, healthy skin underneath to absorb the ingredients that actually make a difference.

One product I've been reaching for lately is the Saint Louve 12% AHA/BHA Exfoliating Serum. It gently resurfaces the skin, encouraging healthy cell turnover while leaving the complexion brighter and smoother.

I follow that with the Saint Louve Vitamin B3, B5 & Hyaluronic Acid Hydrating Serum, which [floods the skin with hydration](/beauty-style/skin-care/difference-between-hydrating-moisturising-products/) before makeup. It's exactly the kind of product that creates the healthy, light-reflective canvas I'm always trying to achieve.

If someone's skin is feeling particularly dry, which is incredibly common after 40, I'll often finish with Saint Louve La Soie Crème. It's rich enough to deeply nourish mature skin but absorbs beautifully, leaving the complexion soft, supple and comfortable.

## Why Foundation Looks Different Today

One of the biggest changes I've witnessed throughout my career is the evolution of foundation itself.

When I first started as a makeup artist, if I wanted skin to look luminous, I had to create it myself. I still remember squeezing Revlon Skinlights into my Napoleon foundations over 25 years ago because naturally radiant foundations simply didn't exist.

Today, complexion products are completely different.

They're thinner in texture, lighter on the skin and often infused with skincare ingredients. Think [serum foundations](/beauty-style/skin-care/12-great-new-foundations-and-concealers-with-real-skincare-benefits/), skin tints and foundation drops that allow your own skin to shine through rather than covering it up.

That's exactly where modern makeup has shifted.

So rather than telling women to throw away everything they own and start again, I encourage them to rethink how they use what they already have.

If a favourite foundation has started looking a little flat, I don't necessarily replace it.

Sometimes I'll simply mix in something nourishing like a face oil or luminous drops. Lately, I've been reaching for the Basics by B Liquid Glow Drops. Just one or two drops mixed through foundation instantly softens a matte finish and brings life back into the skin without making it look shiny.

It's one of those tiny tweaks that completely modernises the way foundation looks.

Don't replace your foundation - change the way you wear it.

## Less Product. Better Placement.

One of the biggest misconceptions I see is that looking fresher requires wearing more makeup.

The opposite is usually true.

The women who leave my chair looking the most refreshed almost always leave wearing less foundation than when they arrived.

Less powder.

Better placement.

Cream textures where appropriate.

A softer, more lifted blush placement.

And makeup that's working with their skin instead of trying to cover it.

It's the biggest lightbulb moment at our Kerrie X Simone events.

"I still look like me."

That's exactly the point.

My goal has never been to make women look more made-up.

It's to help them look like the freshest, healthiest version of themselves.

## The Takeaway

After 37 years, I've learnt that beautiful makeup isn't about chasing trends.

It's about understanding that your skin will continue to evolve - and allowing your makeup to evolve with it.

I don't want people to notice your foundation.

I want them to notice your skin.
