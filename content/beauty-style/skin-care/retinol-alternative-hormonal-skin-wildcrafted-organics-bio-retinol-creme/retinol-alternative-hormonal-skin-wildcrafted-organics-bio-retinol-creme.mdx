<QuickAnswer question="What can I use instead of retinol for hormonal, sensitised skin?">
Look for a bio-retinol such as ALGAKTIV RetinART, derived from microalgae rather than synthesised from vitamin A, paired with bakuchiol, a familiar retinol alternative. Early ingredient-level lab research found both far less irritating than retinol. Wildcrafted Organics' Bio Retinol Crème combines the two with a Juveleven peptide.
</QuickAnswer>

I woke up at 3am last week hot. Properly throw-the-doona-off hot, which might not be weird for some people but I’ve been a cold sleeper my entire life. I lay there for the better part of an hour waiting to fall back asleep and doing the maths on what else has shifted lately.

My skin's been on the list for a while now too. Every time I've reached for vitamin A this year, retinol, retinal, doesn't matter the form, it's turned bumpy and irritated within days. I don't think it's a verdict on retinol itself. I think my skin is sensitised right now, hormonally, in the same way my sleep apparently is, and pushing through it felt like a bad idea.

<PullQuote>Retinol's always been the gold standard, so losing access to it felt like a real penalty.</PullQuote>

I wasn't ready to give up on actives altogether though. So I went looking for something that could do what retinol does without doing what retinol is currently doing to me.

That's how I found [Wildcrafted Organics](https://wildcraftedorganics.com.au/) latest launch. The brand’s founder, Nicole had sent through some information on her Bio Retinol Crème, and I was intrigued before I'd even opened the jar (which I was pleased to see is a proper 50g Miron glass one, the kind that actually looks like it belongs on a shelf rather than hiding in a drawer.)

Here's the short version of what ingredients to actually look for if you're in the same position: the formula pairs ALGAKTIV RetinART, and it’s a bio-retinol derived from microalgae rather than synthesised from vitamin A, with bakuchiol, the more familiar retinol alternative most of us have already tried in one form or another. Both have had serious lab interest for gentleness, tolerability work on the raw ingredients found them far less irritating than retinol while still promising on texture and tone. That's early, ingredient-level research though, not proof about this particular cream, so I went in curious rather than convinced.

The cream itself is a pleasure to use, which is always important when you’re dealing with a sahara-dry [perimenopausal](/beauty-style/skin-care/how-to-prime-your-skin-for-peri-and-menopause) dermis. It's hydrating rather than thin and watery, sinks in properly, and doesn't leave that tight, waiting-for-the-sting feeling I've come to associate with anything labelled active. Three weeks in, my skin has stayed satisfyingly calm the whole time.
