<QuickAnswer question="What is Saint Louve skincare?">
Saint Louve is an Australian skincare range formulated by Elly Seymour, a registered nurse and skin specialist with 15 years of clinical experience. She started developing it in 2020 and spent more than two years working with cosmetic chemists before launch. The range covers cleansers, serums and moisturisers.
</QuickAnswer>

<GiftNote vendor="Saint Louve" />

There's a particular kind of trust that comes from a skincare brand built by someone who actually touches skin for a living. Not a founder with a good Instagram and a manufacturer on speed dial, but someone who's spent years standing over other people's faces, watching what actually calms redness, what actually clogs pores, what actually works when the marketing copy is stripped away.

## Who is Elly Seymour?

That's Elly Seymour. Before Saint Louve existed, she was a registered nurse and skin specialist with fifteen years in clinical skincare, the kind of experience that means she's seen what a bad ingredient combination does to real skin, repeatedly, up close. She started formulating Saint Louve in 2020, and it took more than two years of development with cosmetic chemists before the range launched. That's not a brand timeline built for a content calendar. That's someone taking her time because she knew what she wanted to get right.

## What is the Saint Louve range like to use?

I tried the whole range, cleanser through to the serums, and the thing that struck me first wasn't a single ingredient. It was how considered the whole thing felt. The packaging is minimal, almost clinical, no wasted flourish, and once you start using the products you realise that restraint runs right through the formulations too. Nothing feels thrown in for the label. Every ingredient combination reads like it was chosen by someone who actually knew what she was doing, because she did.

## Where does a nurse-formulated routine start?

With the cleanser. The Facial Cleansing Oil is the one I've reached for most. It's the kind of cleanser that makes you reconsider what a cleanser is for, properly dissolving the day rather than just foaming it away, and my skin never feels stripped afterwards. The Facial Cleansing Gel is in the rotation too, a lighter, second-step follow-up rather than a stand-alone strip-everything-back cleanse.

## What do the Saint Louve serums do?

There are four, and they each have a job. The 15% Vitamin C Lustrum Serum is the brightening and firming one, with ferulic acid and vitamin E alongside. The 12% AHA BHA Exfoliating Serum is the clearing and renewing one. The 0.25% Encapsulated Retinol Serum is the night-time one.

Then there's the Vitamin B3, B5 & Hyaluronic Acid Hydrating Serum: niacinamide (that's Vitamin B3), panthenol (Vitamin B5) and hyaluronic acid, layered together. It's not a flashy formula. It's a well-built one: independent ingredient analysis from [Skincarisma](https://skincarisma.com/products/saint-louve/vitamin-b3-b5-hyaluronic-acid-hydrating-serum) rates it Excellent, 98 out of 100, and lists it as fragrance-free and reef-safe, with an ingredient list that looks well suited to dry and sensitive skin. That's not brand copy. That's an outside body checking the ingredient list line by line and coming back impressed.

> *Ed's note: New to serums? Our guide to [how to self-prescribe your serums](/beauty-style/skin-care/how-to-self-prescribe-your-serums) is a good place to start.*

A word of caution on the retinol. It's potent and, as with any retinol, you may notice some reaction when you start. Ease in slowly: use it every two or three nights, buffer it with moisturiser if you need to, and build tolerance gradually.

## Which Saint Louve moisturiser should you try?

The La Soie Crème, Saint Louve's newest launch, is the one I keep going back to for how it actually feels on skin. It's the kind of moisturiser that makes the rest of your routine feel like it was worth doing. The Clarity Crème is the other one to know, made for oily, breakout-prone or congested skin.
