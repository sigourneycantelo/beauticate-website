## Why can hair dye cause itching, burning or irritation?

Hair colour is a chemical process, and we're asking the hair and scalp to work together during that process. Some ingredients used in permanent colour can cause sensitivity, irritation or, in some cases, an allergic reaction.

It can come down to the condition of the scalp itself or any changes within the body, such as menopause, excessive stress or just that time of the month for women. If your scalp is already dry, compromised, inflamed or particularly sensitive, you may notice more discomfort during a colour service.

I always say that understanding your client's scalp is just as important as understanding their hair.

## What's the difference between a sensitive scalp, irritation and a true hair dye allergy?

They're actually three quite different things.

A sensitive scalp is generally one that is more reactive than others and may not happen all the time. This means it's not really the colour ingredients, it's how your scalp is at the time. You might experience tightness, tingling or discomfort, even without a visible reaction.

Irritation is usually a direct response to something in the colour formula or the colouring process. It can cause stinging, redness or a burning sensation.

An allergic reaction is different. It's an immune response to a particular ingredient, and symptoms can include itching, swelling, redness, a rash or even blistering. Importantly, an allergic reaction can develop after you've used a product previously without any problems.

## Are ammonia-free, organic or natural hair dyes necessarily gentler?

Not necessarily, and I think this is a really important conversation.

Just because something is labelled natural, organic or ammonia-free doesn't automatically mean it's safer or gentler for everyone. Ammonia is only one part of a colour formulation. There are other ingredients involved in making permanent colour work, and those ingredients can also cause sensitivity or irritation.

What I look at is the entire formulation, not just one ingredient on the label.

Pure Perfection is refreshing because it's been developed with a very considered approach to its formulation, removing several ingredients that clients are increasingly asking us about, while still delivering the performance we expect from professional permanent colour.

## Which hair dye ingredients do people most commonly react to?

One of the best-known ingredients associated with hair colour allergies is PPD (paraphenylenediamine), which is commonly found in permanent hair colour. PTD (toluene-2,5-diamine) is another related colour ingredient that can cause allergic reactions.

Other ingredients worth being aware of include persulfates, which are often found in lightening products, hydrogen peroxide and certain fragrance ingredients. Some can cause irritation, while others may trigger an allergic response.

This is where Pure Perfection is particularly interesting for me. It's formulated without PPD/PTD, ammonia or fragrance, among other ingredients.

## How should a colourist approach a consultation for someone with a sensitive scalp?

For me, the consultation is everything. I never want to jump straight into applying colour without understanding my client sitting in my chair.

I ask questions about their previous colour experiences, whether they've ever experienced itching, burning or irritation, whether they've had any known allergies and what their scalp is like day to day. I also want to know about their hair history, the condition of their hair and what they're hoping to achieve.

If someone has experienced a previous reaction, I take that seriously. Every client is different, and a beautiful colour result should never come at the expense of their comfort or wellbeing.

## What should you do if you've reacted to hair colour before?

First things first, don't ignore it and don't try to push through another colour appointment.

If you've experienced a reaction, stop using that colour and speak with your GP or a dermatologist. They may recommend professional allergy testing, such as patch testing, to identify the ingredient responsible. I'd much rather a client take the time to understand their sensitivity than risk another reaction.

It's also important to tell your colourist about any previous reactions, even if they happened years ago. That information can make a real difference to how we approach your service.

## How important is a patch test before hair colour?

It's incredibly important. I would never underestimate it. If you've experienced any sensitivity with permanent colour before, ask for a patch test.

Patch testing is designed to help identify whether someone may have an allergic reaction to a hair colour product before we proceed with a full service. One thing I really want clients to understand is that having used a colour before without a reaction doesn't mean you'll never react to it in the future.

## What should you ask your colourist before your appointment?

I always encourage clients to have an open conversation with their colourist. Never feel silly asking questions about what we're putting on your hair and scalp. I'd suggest asking over the phone before your appointment.

Ask what type of colour brand they're using. Ask whether the formula contains ammonia, PPD/PTD or fragrance, and whether there's an alternative if you have a sensitive scalp. Ask whether you should have a patch test before your appointment, and whether you should wash your hair before you come in.

If your scalp is irritated, sunburnt, broken or inflamed, let your colourist know and postpone the service until it's healthy again. And most importantly, be honest about your history. We can create beautiful colour, but the relationship between a client and their colourist should always start with trust.

For me, the future of hair colour is about more than just the shade we create. It's about understanding the science, respecting the hair and scalp, and making sure our clients feel just as good during the service as they do when they leave the salon. That means having options.
