<PullQuote>The question I get, is always some version of the same thing: "just tell me what to buy."</PullQuote>

There's a question I get asked constantly. In DMs, in WhatsApp chats, at dinner parties, at school pickup. Sometimes from strangers, sometimes from women I've known for twenty years. The question is always some version of the same thing: *just tell me what to buy.*

They don't want a mood board or a list of forty things they'll screenshot and never look at again. They want to know what I would actually choose. The LED mask I'd actually buy. The candle that's actually worth it. The fragrance that's been sitting on my bathroom shelf for three years, not because I forgot about it, but because I keep reaching for it.

<PullQuote>When I was at TV Week, I had to rub bronzing cream on Chris Hemsworth's chest during a cover shoot because the makeup artist had gone home.</PullQuote>

I've been writing about beauty and wellness for twenty-six years. My first beauty writing gig was at an indie magazine called *Your Destiny* when I was 18. From there, TV Week, where I had my own beauty and fashion page (and where I once had to rub bronzing cream on Chris Hemsworth's chest during a cover shoot because the makeup artist had gone home. Someone had to do it). Then New Woman, then Glamour, then Vogue Australia for the better part of a decade. Then Beauticate, which I founded in 2014 because I wanted to build something more personal than a magazine and more honest than the algorithm-driven beauty internet we were all starting to drown in. And for all of that time, the most useful thing I have apparently ever done is be the person my friends text when they need to know where to buy a good candle.

So we built a shop.

Then we rebuilt the entire website.

I'll explain.

![Sigourney Cantelo](/content/sigourneys-edit/beauticate-shop-launch/sigourney-1.jpg)

## The coffee that started it

It took a long time to get here, and the reason it took so long is that I kept hitting the same wall: logistics. I knew what the shop should be. I didn't know how to make it work without a warehouse, a fulfilment team and a level of capital I didn't have. We launched our Top 100 Products list back in 2018, and I watched it drive thousands of clicks to external brand websites, losing my precious readers in the process. Our Sacred Six edits on Instagram would sell products out. The Beautiful Inside podcast would convert listeners into customers for the founders we'd featured. But there was nowhere for any of that to land that was ours.

Then I had coffee with Nicole O'Neil, the founder of [Bon Wellness](https://beauticate-website.vercel.app/shop/collections/bon-patch). She asked why I hadn't built a shop. I told her: too complicated, too much stock, too much risk. And she said: have you looked at Modern Dropship?

<PullQuote>Finally I could curate the products I wanted to feature and the brands would ship your order directly from their own warehouse.</PullQuote>

I went home and looked it up. The technology had caught up with what I'd wanted to do for years. I could curate the products I believed in and the brands would ship directly from their own warehouse. No stock. No fulfilment headaches. The editorial model I'd always imagined, finally technically possible.

That was almost a year ago. What followed was not easy.

## What actually happened (part one)

The short version: I hired a developer to build the shop. They built it. And then they locked me out of my own website, because they'd built it in a way that made it impossible for me to make changes myself. I had to start again.

What I discovered, rebuilding in Shopify myself, is that I didn't need a developer in the first place. Shopify is remarkable. It has a built-in AI called Sidekick that I've started calling Beauticate's MVP, because it has quietly saved me more times than I can count. If you've been putting off building something in e-commerce because you think it's too technical: it is less hard than you think. I am living proof.

So the shop was built. The brands were onboarded. We were days from launch.

## What actually happened (part two)

The night before we were ready to go, my husband Damien said something I probably should have seen coming.

*You know how you've always wanted the shop to live inside your website? Well, we can do it now.*

He explained the setup. The shop sitting inside Beauticate, not as a separate destination but as part of the same editorial world I'd been building for twelve years. It was everything I'd wanted.

It would also mean rebuilding the entire site from scratch.

Twelve years of content. Seventeen hundred articles. Thousands of images. I said yes.

I know.

What followed was five weeks I won't forget quickly. Waking most mornings at five or six, working until my eyes were burning, stumbling into bed and starting again the next day. The migration scrambled more articles than I care to count. Broken images. Floating URLs. Garbled text. Articles in entirely the wrong place, looking nothing like they should. On top of onboarding all the brands for the shop, getting their listings right and building a new site from scratch, I was fixing twelve years of content one article at a time.

Some days it felt like bailing out a boat with a teaspoon.

But here we are. And honestly? I'm proud of what we've built. Let me show you around.

<PullQuote>Every product on it has been tested, considered, chosen by me, by the Beauticate Collective, or by our guest curators. Nothing is there because a brand paid to be there.</PullQuote>

## How it works

Beauticate Shop works a little differently from most retailers. Rather than holding stock in a single warehouse, each brand we work with ships your order directly from theirs. It means your products come from the people who made them. It means the brands stay independent and we stay editorial. And it means that when you shop a curated edit, your order may arrive in several beautiful parcels, sometimes a few days apart.

We think it's worth it. The brands keep their craft. We keep our independence. And every order helps us keep making the editorial work that's brought you here.

### 02 — Ask Sig

This one I'm particularly excited about. Ask Sig is an AI-powered beauty, wellness and style concierge that lives on every page of the site. It draws on twelve years of Beauticate editorial and the full shop catalogue to answer your questions, recommend products and surface relevant articles. In my voice. You can ask it anything: what SPF should I wear in winter, what's the best retinol for sensitive skin, where should I go in Bali. It knows what I know. It's the friend who happens to know everything, available any time.

### 03 — Beautiful Inside Podcast Hub

The Beautiful Inside podcast has a proper home now, with a mood-based filter so you can find exactly what you need. Confidence. Reinvention. Health. Healing. Perimenopause. Beauty and Skin. Business. Motherhood. Reset. Browse by what you're going through, not just by episode number.

### 04 — Destinations: Start With a Feeling

The travel section is built around how I actually choose a holiday. Not by destination, but by feeling. Six mood tiles: Girls Trip, Reset and Heal, Switch Off, Explore a City, Family Adventure, Romantic Escapes. Each links through to a curated edit of places, stays and stories. Start with how you want to feel when you get there.

### 05 — The Beauty and Wellness Directory

A searchable directory of spas, salons, skin clinics, bathhouses, retreats and wellness venues across Australia, filterable by state and venue type. The trusted list I've been building in my head for years, finally somewhere you can actually search it.

### 06 — About Beauticate and The Collective

The about page has been completely rebuilt, with a full brand timeline, Sigourney's founder letter and dedicated pages for every member of the Beauticate Collective.

The Beauticate Collective is a standing group of editors, facialists, doctors, nutritionists and beauty experts whose taste shapes the shop alongside mine.

Kate Waterhouse on style. Jocelyn Petroni on skin. Rae Morris on makeup. Michelle Bridges on wellness. Fourteen editors and experts, each with their own page.

Plus more across every category we cover. You'll see their picks throughout the shop, and over time you'll hear from them directly through guest edits, columns and podcast appearances.

The most credible editorial board I've ever worked with, and I worked at Vogue.

### 07 — From the Archive

Twenty years of print journalism, finally in one place. Thirteen Vogue Australia features including Lady Gaga, Rihanna, Claudia Schiffer, Cate Blanchett and Jo Malone, plus pieces from *Studio*, *Body+Soul* and *New Woman*, with links to the original PDF scans. If you've ever wondered what I was writing before Beauticate existed, here it is.

### 08 — Press

Twenty-one-plus press features, podcast appearances and brand collaboration highlights. Vogue, Marie Claire, Daily Telegraph, Daily Mail and more. Included here not to boast, but because it answers the question I also get asked often: who is Beauticate, really? This is who.

### 09 — Beauticate Insiders

Every current Beauticate subscriber becomes a founding Insider. What that means in practice: early access to new edits and drops, sampling opportunities, invitations to events and first look at everything we build next. The programme is evolving, but founding members are the women who showed up first. That's you.

## A note on what you might find broken

The new site is live. The shop is open. And if you spot something that looks wrong — a broken image, a scrambled article, a link that goes nowhere — please go easy on me. Five weeks of five AM starts got us a long way. It didn't get us all the way.

If you find something, I want to know. Email us at hello@beauticate.com and we'll fix it.

## What I need from you

Tell me what brands you think should be in the shop that aren't. Tell me what's missing from your routine that you wish I'd curate. Tell me what doesn't work. Your feedback is how we decide what to build next.

The shop has been nearly a year in the making, with a surprise five-week website rebuild tucked in at the end. There were moments I wanted to walk away from all of it.

But I kept coming back to that original question: *Just tell me what to buy.*

Here's my answer. [Come and have a look.](https://beauticate-website.vercel.app/shop)
