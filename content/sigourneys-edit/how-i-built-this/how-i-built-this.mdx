My husband has excellent timing. The night before we were ready to launch, he walked into the room and said: you know how you've always wanted the shop to live inside the website? Well, we can do it now.

He was not wrong. It was everything I'd wanted. The shop sitting inside Beauticate, a destination woven into the same editorial world I'd spent twelve years building. One ecosystem. One experience. Content and commerce finally in the same place.

It would also mean rebuilding the entire site from scratch.

Twelve years of content. Seventeen hundred articles. Thousands of images. I said yes. How could I not? If I'd known what was coming, maybe I would have thought about it a little longer.

What followed was six weeks that I will never forget. Waking most mornings at 5am (weekends included), working until my eyes were burning, stumbling into bed and starting again before dawn. The migration scrambled more articles than I could count. Broken images. Floating URLs. Garbled text. Articles in entirely the wrong place, looking nothing like they should. On top of onboarding all the brands for the shop, getting their listings right and building a new site from scratch, I was fixing over 1,700 articles.

<PullQuote>Some days it felt like bailing out a boat with a teaspoon.</PullQuote>

But I need to back up. Because this story actually starts about a year earlier, with a coffee.

---

## The coffee that started it

I had coffee with Nicole O'Neil (of Real Housewives fame), the fabulous founder of [Bon Wellness](/shop/collections/bon-patch), and we were talking about e-comm, as you do.

I was telling her how shopping guides we create are some of our most-read content. We launched our Top 100 Products list back in 2018, and I watched it drive thousands of clicks to external brand websites, losing my readers in the process. Then we did the Top 50 Skincare Products and the Sacred 60. They were beautiful to look at and useful to read, but we needed to make them more shoppable. Our Sacred Six edits on Instagram would sell products out. The Beautiful Inside podcast would convert listeners into customers for the founders we'd featured. But there was nowhere for any of that to land that was ours.

Nicole asked why I hadn't built a shop. I gave her the usual answer: too complicated, too much stock, too much risk. She asked me if I'd looked at Modern Dropship.

I went home and looked it up that evening. The technology had finally caught up with what I'd wanted to do for years. I could curate the products I believed in and the brands would ship directly from their own warehouses. No stock. No fulfillment headaches. The editorial model I'd always imagined, finally technically possible.

That was almost a year ago.

---

## Sell the dream before you lose your nerve

So I set about contacting the brands I wanted to feature, did 6 months of daily meetings, selling the dream before I even built the product. I knew I could curate the best of the best and tell stories that would sell them. I've been doing this more than half of my life.

My first beauty writing gig was at an indie magazine called *Your Destiny* when I was 18. From there, *TV Week*, where I had my own beauty and fashion page (and where I once had to rub bronzing cream on Chris Hemsworth's chest during a cover shoot because the makeup artist had gone home. True story). Then *New Woman*, then *Glamour*, then *Vogue Australia* for the better part of a decade. Then Beauticate, which I founded in 2014 because I wanted to build something more personal than a magazine and more honest than the algorithm-driven beauty internet we were all starting to drown in.

Beauticate has always shared the best products and how to use them. That kind of intel is literally DNA of the site, so it felt like the logical next step for our brand: creating an eco-system where our readers can stay within our universe and discover the best products that will ultimately make their lives easier - and more beautiful.

![Sigourney Cantelo](/content/sigourneys-edit/beauticate-shop-launch/sigourney-1.jpg)

I was shocked by how many brands said yes, before I even had a proof of concept. Those early wins gave me the confidence and energy to keep going and to fork out the money for the development of the shop. First mistake.

---

## When I spent thousands building a site I couldn't use

The short version: I hired a developer to build the shop. They built it. And then they locked me out of my own website, because they'd built it in a way that made it nearly impossible for me to make changes myself.

I had to start again.

What I discovered, rebuilding in Shopify myself, is that I didn't need a developer in the first place. Shopify is remarkable. It has a built-in AI agent called Sidekick that I've started calling Beauticate's MVP, because it has quietly saved me more times than I can count. If you've been putting off building something in e-commerce because you think it's too technical, it is less hard than you think. I am living proof.

The shop was built. The first twenty brands were live. I hadn't slept properly in months. We were days from launch.

And then Damien had his big idea.

## What we built

I'll let [the full site tour](/sigourneys-edit/beauticate-shop-launch) speak for itself. But here's what I want you to know: everything that's new on this site, including the shop, Ask Sig, the podcast hub, the directory, the archive, the Collective pages, was built during those six months of early mornings and lost weekends. It nearly *broke* me.

My family would watch TV every night while I would sit on my laptop feeling guilty but also knowing I just had to birth this thing I'd been working so hard on for the last 6 months of my life.

<PullQuote>I'm more proud of it than anything I've built since I launched Beauticate in 2014.</PullQuote>

The brands in the shop are there because I believe in them. Many are female-founded. Most are independent. All of them said yes before they'd even seen what we were building, and *that* told me everything I needed to know. There are more coming. Watch this space.
