<QuickAnswer question="What happened at the first Beauticate Collective lunch?">
The first Beauticate Collective lunch was held on Wednesday 14 October at Lua, Luke Nguyen's restaurant at Sydney Fish Market. It celebrated the Beauticate relaunch and the Beauticate Shop. Eleven guests shared a long lunch, watched oysters dressed with flaming wagyu tallow and took home gift bags from shop brands.
</QuickAnswer>

At 6am on Wednesday I was in bed, refreshing the weather app like it owed me money. Sixty per cent chance of showers. Then forty. Then seventy. (Refreshing it didn't change the forecast, it turns out.)

I'd pictured this lunch for months. A long table on the harbour at Sydney Fish Market, sun on the water, nobody in any hurry to leave. It had taken that long just to find one date that suited everyone in the Beauticate Collective. These are women with clinics, businesses, columns, podcasts and books on the go. Getting them all in one place felt a little like herding very accomplished cats.

So when the Lua team quietly moved us inside, I had a small private wobble. Then I sat down. It was warm, light-filled and comfortable, with the bay still glittering through the windows. Nobody was cold. Nobody was clutching a napkin against the wind. They were right. I was wrong. Lunch was saved.

## Why the Collective needed a lunch

The [Beauticate Collective](/beauty-style/beauty-tips/beauticate-collective-editors-experts) is the group of experts, writers and founders whose voices shape so much of what we publish. I chose every one of them deliberately. Yet many of them had never met. They'd read each other's pieces, shared each other's posts and seen each other's names in my inbox, but they'd never sat at the same table.

I wanted to change that. Partly to build a community of women who raise each other up. And partly, as I confessed in my speech, because I love them all and wanted an excuse to spend more time together.

There was plenty to celebrate too. Beauticate has just relaunched after twelve years, and the [Beauticate Shop](/shop) is open. It felt like the right moment to raise a glass.

## Lunch at Lua

[Lua](https://luasydney.com.au/) sits right on the water at the new Sydney Fish Market, looking out over Blackwattle Bay. The name means fire in Vietnamese, and the charcoal hearth at its centre makes the point for you. Luke Nguyen was out of town on the day, but the Lua team looked after us beautifully. Huge thanks to Shelley Mitchell, Head of Events at Doltone Hospitality Group, and Nina Milazzo, who made it all happen.

Around the table were [Kate Waterhouse](/author/kate-waterhouse), [Jocelyn Petroni](/author/jocelyn-petroni), [Dr Leanne Girgis](/author/dr-leanne-girgis), [Shentel Lee](/author/shentel-lee), [Simone Aspinall](/author/simone-aspinall), [Kerrie Gentle](/author/kerrie-gentle), [Kristin Rawson](/author/kristin-rawson), [Brooke Stevenson](/author/brooke-stevenson) and [Michelle Bridges](/author/michelle-bridges), joined by Elle Halliwell and Dr Nel Wijetunga. [Rae Morris](/author/rae-morris), [Camilla Thompson](/author/camilla-thompson), [Monique McMahon](/author/monique-mcmahon) and [Jacqueline Alwill](/author/jacqueline-alwill) were missed. Next time.

We started with Champagne, then moved on to rosé and cocktails, which loosened everyone up nicely.

Then Giovanni, Lua's venue manager, led us up to the bar for the bit I'm still thinking about. One of the chefs held a flambadou, a cast-iron cone, in the coals until it glowed, then filled it with wagyu beef tallow. It caught fire and dripped in flaming beads over freshly shucked oysters. They hissed. Every phone in the group went up at once. (Mine included. I'm not above it.)

Back at the table, the food kept coming. Kingfish crudo in fragrant coconut milk with rice paddy herb, cool and bright. The wagyu pho tartare, one of Lua's most-ordered dishes, which somehow tastes exactly like a bowl of pho without the bowl. A chicken and cabbage salad, crunchy with pickles and sharp with Vietnamese mint. Charred eggplant with pickled daikon, shallots and pine nuts.

Then the wagyu sirloin landed, blushing pink, with a pho jus and a Vietnamese salsa verde I'd happily have drunk. The table cheered. Not for the chef, though he deserved it. For the protein. Every woman there is trying to eat more of it, and here it was, sliced and fanned out in front of us. Nobody left hungry.

A few of the women had also chosen candles from [Lumira](/shop/brands/lumira).

## The bit that mattered most

I said a few words between courses about why I started the Collective and what I want Beauticate to be. A trusted friend who's done the research and can say, "Here you go. These are the good ones."

I told them they're the reason Beauticate has authority, credibility and heart. I meant every word.

But the moment I'll keep came after the speech, when I sat back and just listened. Breathwork at one end of the table. Perimenopause in the middle. A spirited debate about supplements somewhere near the eggplant. And beauty, of course, everywhere. Phone numbers changing hands. Women who'd been names in each other's feeds that morning, talking like old friends by the time the plates were cleared.

That's my favourite thing in the world. Bringing good people together, then getting out of the way.

## Book Lua

Lua is at Sydney Fish Market, 1 Bridge Road, Glebe. It's open from 11am daily, until 9pm Sunday to Thursday and 10pm Friday and Saturday. [Book a table on Lua's website](https://www.sevenrooms.com/explore/luarestaurantbar/reservations/create/search/). Groups of eight or more book through the [group bookings page](https://luasydney.com.au/group-bookings.html), where a banquet menu is required. Banquets start from $79 per person, though menus and prices can change.
