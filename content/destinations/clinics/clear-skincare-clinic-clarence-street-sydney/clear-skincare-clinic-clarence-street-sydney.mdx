I have a confession: I put off laser hair removal for years. The idea of someone zapping my underarms with a laser while I lay there in a paper gown was not exactly my idea of a relaxing lunch break. But when I finally walked into Clear Skincare on Clarence Street, I wondered what had taken me so long.

The clinic sits at Shop 2, 171 Clarence Street — a short walk from both Town Hall and Wynyard stations, which makes it dangerously convenient for anyone who works in the CBD. Stepping in from the bustle of the street, the pace shifts immediately. The French-inspired interior is sleek and modern: blonde wood floors, crisp white walls and a considered fit-out that feels welcoming without veering into cold clinical territory. I sank into a sea foam linen chair in reception and filled out my consultation form, already feeling calmer than I had all morning.

## The Backstory

Clear Skincare was founded in 1995 by Dr Philippa McCaffery, a medical professional widely respected for her work in women's health and adult acne. She pioneered the use of cosmetic lasers in Australia during the nineties and built what began as a single clinic into a national network. In 2018, the business was acquired by Australian Pharmaceutical Industries (API) for $127.4 million; Wesfarmers subsequently acquired API in 2022, folding Clear Skincare into its Wesfarmers Health division alongside Priceline Pharmacy. That corporate backing has allowed the brand to invest heavily in new technology and expand to more than 90 clinics across Australia and New Zealand — a long way from the original 40 I remember reading about — while maintaining the clinical standards Dr McCaffery established.

It is reassuring, honestly. Whether you visit a Clear Skincare in Melbourne, Brisbane or here on Clarence Street, you get the same bright, clean aesthetic, the same medical-grade technology and the same rigorously trained clinicians. That consistency is what drew me here in the first place.

## What's on the Menu

The treatment offering has evolved considerably since the early laser-only days. Today the Clarence Street clinic's menu includes:

- **HydraFacial** — the cult multi-step facial that cleanses, extracts and hydrates in one sitting
- **MediPeels** — clinical-grade chemical peels tailored to individual skin concerns
- **LED Light Therapy** — targeted wavelengths to calm inflammation, stimulate collagen or combat acne
- **Microdermabrasion** — mechanical exfoliation for a smoother, more refined texture
- **Microneedling** — controlled micro-injuries to trigger the skin's natural repair process
- **Sylfirm RF Needling** — radiofrequency-assisted needling for pigmentation, redness and skin tightening
- **Signature Acne Treatments** — building on Dr McCaffery's original protocols for stubborn breakouts
- **Laser Hair Removal** — still one of the brand's most popular services, now using the latest generation of devices
- **Cosmetic Injectables** — anti-wrinkle and dermal filler treatments administered by qualified practitioners

I will admit, looking at the full menu, I am already plotting my next visit for a HydraFacial.

## My Experience

After being introduced to my clinician, I was shown into a crisp, white treatment room. We had a quick chat about my skin goals — specifically, getting rid of the hair under my arms — and then she stepped out so I could change. I had been advised to shave the area beforehand, and once I had donned some rather fetching protective glasses, I lay back on the bed with my arms gingerly behind my head.

My clinician wiped the area clean, marked it up with a highlighter pen and reached for the laser. I noticed she was using a device with a built-in cooling tip to help manage discomfort, plus an additional cooling device that chills the skin even further. I braced myself — but honestly, it felt so much less painful than the last laser treatment I had had years ago. The technology has clearly come a long way.

The whole thing was over within about ten minutes. She applied a cooling gel and sent me off with an aftercare pack including post-laser gel, an exfoliating cloth and a microdermabrasion cream with glycolic acid. I could not believe how quick and painless it was.

Off to the side of reception, a digital skin bar caught my eye on the way out — it invites you to sit down, take a quiz and learn about your skin before committing to any treatment. Education clearly rates highly here, which I appreciate. I like a clinic that wants me to understand my skin, not just hand over my credit card.
