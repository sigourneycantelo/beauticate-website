#### The Double Bay skinstitution is named after its owner, the Hungarian facialist Eva Karpati, who has been a mainstay of the local beauty scene since the eighties and has now been at it for more than 30 years.

## Inside the Bay Street Building

Five treatment rooms in a sleek grey building on Bay Street, in the middle of Double Bay's mecca for preening. You step in off a busy street into quiet: a water feature tinkling, a jumbo candle doing serious work on the stairwell, and a waiting room done in a cosy Asian-spa register where the receptionist pours tea from a silver tray.

[Karpati](http://www.karpatimedispa.com.au/) has the high-tech thing going on without the sterility that usually comes with it. Then you're handed a very thorough form, and you understand these people mean business.

## The Pollogen Geneo+

Treatment rooms come with a satin gown, a fuzzy throw and an electric blanket, which is a combination no medispa has any right to get so correct.

The newest machine is the Pollogen Geneo+, with multiple heads for exfoliation, infusion and ultrasound. "This is called a super facial and it's very big all over the world," Eva says. "It's owned by a company who supply the medical industry, they have several patents now with the aesthetic industry."

Worth knowing before you book it: it isn't performed during pregnancy.

## Machines, and Rather a Lot of Them

Eva and her husband are distributors of various laser and IPL machines, and of Resultime by Collin Paris, which they supply to medispas and clinics around the country. They also have their own skin and haircare range, Karpati 100% Natural, which promises no petro-chemicals. The Marine Enzyme Powder and the tinted Blemish Cover Up are the standouts.

## Eva's Approach

Her trademark facials combine traditional European technique, and she's known for thorough extractions, with galvanic, laser, IPL, collagen and vein treatments.

"My theory is you shouldn't terrorise and burn, you should be nice and gentle and encouraging," she says, in a pretty, lilting accent. "I analyse my clients and I give them an individual program, everybody's different. It's a combination of the individual, the age, the genetic background, the skincare, the lifestyle, the blemishes they already have built up there."

She has opinions and she shares them. Microdermabrasion she hates, calling it brutal and unhygienic. Extractions she thinks you should have professionally every four to six weeks, the way they do in Hungary. An hour in her chair is close to a short course in beauty therapy, and the gentle facial is available entirely without the techno-treats.
