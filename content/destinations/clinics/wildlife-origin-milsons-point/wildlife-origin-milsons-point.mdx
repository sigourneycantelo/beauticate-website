Wildlife occupies some seriously prime Kirribilli real estate, and every chair and mirror in the place is angled to know it. The view takes in the arc of the Harbour Bridge, the grassy knolls of Bradfield Park and a long stretch of Sydney's most photographed waterway.

## Jane Wild and the Team

The director is Jane Wild, who has been named Australian Hairdresser of the Year three times. Wildlife Origin is one of three salons in the business, the others being Sogo in Surry Hills and Studio in Milson's Point.

## The Renovation

A recent renovation turned the front windows into sliding doors that cantilever open, which brings the view literally into the salon. The street-side entry was opened up at the same time and now holds a café, tempting locals in with Single Origin and Vietnamese coffee, sharp little salads and desserts of some invention. Violet Crumble muffins, for one.

## Hair

Colour work includes the Shu Uemura Colour Lustre treatment, and it starts the way the good ones do, with a scalp brush-out using a paddle brush in each hand before anything else happens. Mint tea, an afternoon breeze through the open front, and the strong impression of having your hair done on somebody's waterfront balcony.

The basin area is darker and quieter than the floor. The salon washes with specially purified water rather than straight off the mains.

## The Beauty Menu

Daniella runs the beauty side, either in the chair or next door in one of four treatment rooms: Cosmedix and Payot facials, gel manicures, waxing, massage and makeup. Injectables are also available, administered by a visiting cosmetic doctor rather than salon staff.

Wildlife make their own candles, so you can take the smell of the place home.

## Jane Wild on the One-Stop Shop

"It's not just about the hair," Jane Wild says, winding hair around a brush and blasting it with a ghd aura. "It's about having a whole Wildlife experience.

My dream when we first built the salon was to have a one-stop shop. A destination where clients can have hair, beauty, they can have something to eat. Most of my clients are time poor and running to get the kids, or get back to work, and they forget to eat, so it's important they can get everything done at once, because it's precious time that you get to yourself."

Which is the whole argument for the café, made better than any menu could.
