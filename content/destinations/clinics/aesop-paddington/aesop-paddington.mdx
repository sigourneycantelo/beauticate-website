I have always loved shopping at [Aesop](https://www.aesop.com/au/) — the amber bottles, the scent that greets you at the door, the way every store feels like stepping into a small, considered world. But I had never actually had a facial at one of their stores until I visited the Paddington outpost on Glenmore Road, and I am still thinking about it.

The store relocated from its former Oxford Street address to this quieter stretch of Glenmore Road, and from the moment I walked in I could tell the move was deliberate. The space was redesigned by Melbourne practice Clare Cousins Architects, who drew on the geometry and materiality of swimming pools — glazed tiles in watery blue-green hues, warm blonde timber, smooth concrete. There is a stillness to it that pulls you inward. I actually paused on the stairs down to the treatment room and just stood there for a moment, letting the hush settle. It felt less like a beauty counter and more like a private wellness retreat tucked beneath a terrace house.

## My treatment

My facialist began with a deep Parsley Seed Oil cleanse — slow, deliberate strokes across my forehead and jawline that immediately softened the tension I had been carrying all week. She asked about my skin (combination, prone to congestion around my chin, a bit sensitised from retinol) and then set about blending raw ingredients right in front of me: vitamins, antioxidants, plant extracts, all mixed with Aesop products chosen specifically for what my skin needed that day.

What struck me was how tactile the entire experience was. Bases of green clay and fine white China clay were applied in cool, generous layers that drew heat and tightness from my face as they set. I could feel my pores contracting. When my facialist peeled the mask away and began layering moisture back in, my skin drank it up without a hint of irritation — impressive, given how reactive my complexion can be.

The most memorable part, though, was the botanicals. Jasmine, chamomile, and the rare Madagascan *ylang ylang* flower were blended into bespoke combinations and pressed into my skin. The fragrance was extraordinary — warm, green, deeply calming — and the effect was as much olfactory therapy as it was skincare. I left feeling like I had been gently reassembled.

Two treatment lengths are offered: a **60-minute facial at $240** and an **extended 75-minute facial at $300**. I opted for the longer session and would absolutely recommend it — the extra fifteen minutes allowed for a more indulgent mask phase and an unhurried scalp massage that I did not want to end. Both durations are entirely customised, so no two facials will be the same. It is a genuinely individualised experience, and the kind of treatment I would happily return to whenever my skin — or my headspace — needs resetting.
